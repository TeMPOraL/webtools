Trilat Field Survey — manual

Records tape-measured distances between marks (pegs, corners, fence posts) and continuously computes the best-fitting 2D positions by least-squares network adjustment — trilateration only, no angles needed. The first mark becomes the origin (0,0), the second defines the +X axis; the third mark's side (or your plan) fixes the mirror orientation. All data stays on this device.

Field quick-start

  1. Tap FROM → pick or create a mark; same for TO; type the taped distance in metres; hit ＋.
  2. FROM stays selected — keep shooting from the same station to other targets.
  3. Watch the header after every entry: σ should stay at a few mm and residual chips in the Log green.
  4. If a red ⚠ remeasure warning names a pair, re-tape it before moving on.
  5. Open Checks and shoot suggested pairs until redundancy is at least ≈ the number of marks.
  6. Data → Download/Share the .txt before leaving the field.

Planning at home

  • Marks tab: pre-add every mark you intend to set out, optionally with sketch coordinates (metres, any local frame). Planned marks show as dashed squares on the map and appear in the pickers; measurements snap them into place. A plan also decides the mirror orientation of the solution.
  • Background image: 🖼 on the map → Load image (photo of a paper sketch, satellite screenshot, site plan). While Adjust is on, drag moves the image and a two-finger pinch scales and rotates it (mouse: wheel scales, ⟲⟳ rotate) — line it up with your planned or measured marks, then tap Adjust again to lock. ◐ cycles opacity. The image is stored on this device only and is not part of the text export.

Entering measurements

  • Touch: FROM/TO pickers with per-mark measurement counts; ⇄ swaps; comma or dot decimals. You can also tap marks directly on the map — first tap sets FROM (green ring), second sets TO (pink ring).
  • Keyboard (⌨): one line per shot — A B 12.34 full form; B 12.34 uses the sticky FROM; 12.34 repeats the current pair. Enter adds. New names create marks automatically.
  • Measuring the same pair several times is fine — repeats are averaged by the adjustment and add redundancy.
  • Fix a typo by tapping the number in the Log; ✕ deletes (tap twice); ↩ in the header undoes anything.

Reading the map

  • Drag pans, pinch or wheel zooms, double-tap or ⛶ re-fits.
  • Grey lines = measurements · amber = residual > 2σ · red = suspect · blue = latest entry.
  • Blue ellipses = 95% confidence regions of each mark, exaggerated ×N (see legend; ±σ cycles auto/×1/×10/…/off).
  • Dashed cyan lines = suggested check pairs with predicted distance — tap one to preload it into the entry bar.
  • Dashed squares = planned, unmeasured marks · dashed orange circles = weak marks that need more distances.

The numbers

  • redundancy = measurements − unknowns. Negative: not solvable yet. Zero: solvable, but nothing cross-checks your work — a blunder would be invisible. Every extra measurement adds a check and shrinks the ellipses.
  • σ = RMS of residuals ≈ your overall measuring noise. A steel tape on a garden plot should give a few mm; a sudden jump means a bad measurement, not a worse tape.
  • Residual = measured − computed, shown per row in the Log.
  • Suspects are found two ways: residuals far out of family (robust median test), and a leave-one-out scan — "dropping X–Y alone would cut σ …" names the single measurement most worth re-taping.

Data safety

  • Everything autosaves to this browser's localStorage on every keystroke — it survives reloads, tab kills and phone reboots. It does not survive clearing site data, and does not sync between browsers/devices: export the .txt regularly.
  • The export feeds straight into trilat.py; the #!plan/#!mark lines are comments to it. Import accepts the same format (replace or append).
  • Install as an app via the browser menu ("Add to Home screen"). ☀ keeps the screen awake while measuring.

Coordinates are in a local frame in metres. CSV export gives adjusted coordinates; SVG export gives the current map view.
